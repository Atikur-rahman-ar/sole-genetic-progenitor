\section{The polymorphism objection at balanced loci}\label{sec:poly}

\subsection{The objection and its two errors}
The classical form of the polymorphism objection is due to
\citet{Ayala1995}: the HLA loci carry dozens of deeply diverged allelic
lineages, some of which appear to coalesce before the human--chimpanzee
split, and a bottleneck to four gene copies cannot transmit that many
lineages. The objection contains two modeling errors, both documented in
the literature on the objection's own terms \citep{Gauger2012,
GarriganHedrick2003}. First, Ayala's
simulation imposed a bottleneck of \emph{extended duration} --- a population
held small for many generations --- whereas the single-couple claim
requires exactly one generation at $N=2$, whose lineage-retention
properties are those of Proposition~\ref{prop:trans}: transmission
losses are per-generation events, and a one-generation excursion
transmits three of four lineages in expectation (Section~\ref{sec:ret}).
Second, the coalescence-depth estimates at HLA loci do not account for
\emph{convergent evolution}: inter-allelic gene conversion at the MHC,
which occurs at rates far above the genomic background, mimics deep
common ancestry by importing segments between allelic lineages
\citep{GarriganHedrick2003}. The trans-species depth of HLA alleles is
therefore an
upper bound contaminated by an unknown but demonstrably large conversion
contribution, not a clean coalescent measurement; how much of the depth
survives a conversion correction is exactly the lineage-count test of
Section~\ref{sec:roots}.

\subsection{Retention under balancing selection}\label{sec:ret}
At an overdominant locus, lineage extinction is suppressed: a rare allele
spends nearly all its copies in heterozygotes and enjoys the selective
advantage $s$ of the heterozygote, so its extinction hazard scales down by
$s/(2\Ne)$ per generation relative to the neutral rate
\citep{Takahata1990}. An exact
Wright--Fisher simulation of the coupled bottleneck-and-expansion process
--- the expansion phase
sampled generation by generation with the overdominant marginal fitness
$1 + s(1-p)$ acting on each lineage, the plateau with the Takahata
extinction-hazard reduction --- over the parameter sweep
$s \in [0.01, 0.1]$ and $\gamma \in \{1.6, 2.0\}$ yields the expected
retention of the four founder lineages. The result is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hlaret}
E[\text{founder lineages retained}] \;=\; 2.1\text{--}2.5 \ \ \text{of } 4
\end{equation}
at the canonical growth schedule ($\gamma = 1.6$: $2.12$--$2.17$; at the
doubling schedule $\gamma = 2.0$: $2.41$--$2.46$). A coalescent scaling
that omits the earliest generations yields a higher figure; the exact
simulation prices the earliest generations --- where $2\Ne s < 1$ and
selection has not yet established protection --- generation by
generation. The diversity-level consequence is
identical under either treatment because the master equation
\eqref{eq:master} already prices early lineage loss through the hazard
$\Lambda_c$ (the expansion segment of the hazard integral includes the
small-$N$ generations at their full $1/2N$ weight). The balanced-locus
diversity calculation is therefore insensitive to which retention figure
is adopted.

\subsection{Regeneration of allelic richness}
Retained lineages are the seed, not the harvest. At an HLA-scale target of
$L \approx 3.3$ kb with intra-locus gene conversion at $\kappa = 5$ times
the background rate --- a conservative multiplier for the MHC, where
recombination and conversion rates reach one to two orders of magnitude
above the genomic average \citep{GarriganHedrick2003} --- the supply of new
selectable lineages is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hlares}
\sum_t 2N(t)\,\mu L\kappa\,\cdot\,\tfrac{2s}{3}
\;=\; 8{,}705
\end{equation}
new allelic lineages over the canonical $550$ kyr, where $2s/3$ is the
classical establishment probability of a new allele under symmetric
overdominance \citep{Takahata1990,CrowKimura1970}. Observed per-population
HLA-DRB1 diversity is $11$--$28$ defined alleles, with on the order of
$10^2$--$10^3$ lineages worldwide \citep{vonSalome2007}. The regeneration
budget exceeds the observed richness
by two orders of magnitude; the binding constraint is the equilibrium
allele count under overdominance --- which the equilibrium calculation for
the observed selection scales shows is met --- not the mutational supply.
The same arithmetic covers the other
classical balanced loci and the immunoglobulin-family receptors.

\subsection{The lineage-count test}\label{sec:roots}
Regeneration multiplies alleles on the retained backbone; it does not
create independent deep roots. The binding constraint of the polymorphism
objection is therefore not the number of alleles --- that is a supply
question, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:hlares} shows supply is abundant --- but the
number of \emph{pre-split allelic lineages} a locus requires: lineages
whose genealogical depth exceeds anything that can be generated below
$T_0$. The EP class caps this number at the retained founder lineages,
$2.1$--$2.5$ of $4$ per locus (Eq.~\eqref{eq:hlaret}), with the depth of
the retained lineages carried in the founders' copies as priced in the
next subsection. The constraint is decidable rather than rhetorical,
because gene conversion leaves sequence signatures: a conversion tract
imports a segment whose genealogy conflicts with its flanking genealogy,
and such tracts are mappable from whole-genome allelic alignments. The
model's falsifier at balanced loci is accordingly explicit: \emph{a
demonstration that any HLA-class locus requires more than four pre-split
allelic roots after excluding mappable conversion tracts falsifies the EP
class at balanced loci}, whatever the fate of the diversity-level
arguments. The classical claim of dozens of deep lineages
\citep{Ayala1995} predates whole-genome MHC assembly and inter-allelic
conversion mapping; the lineage count under modern data is the quantity
the debate should be conducted on. The symmetry of the position deserves
explicit statement. This paper does not establish that the corrected count
at any HLA-class locus is at or below four --- no such count has been
published under a conversion-tract protocol, and the retention ceiling of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hlaret} is a prediction the data have not yet been shown to
respect, only a ceiling the class imposes. What the analysis establishes
is that the quantity has become countable: full-length allelic alignments
for the HLA loci are public, conversion tracts are mappable from
flanking-genealogy conflicts, and the protocol yields a single integer per
locus to which both sides' claims are exposed at once --- the classical
dozens-of-roots reading, which must survive the correction to stand, and
the EP class, which fails at any locus whose corrected count exceeds its
retention ceiling. The test is per-locus and cumulative, and the
balanced-locus condition of the feasibility theorem (item 8 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) holds only if every balanced locus passes. Until
the counts are run, the evidential standing of the deep-root half is
symmetric --- no corrected count supports the classical reading and none
contradicts the ceiling --- but the burden is not. The dozens-of-roots
claim is the position in need of demonstration, and it has stood for three
decades on depth estimates that the conversion literature itself
discredits as clean coalescent signal; the EP class specifies its ceiling
in advance and names the protocol that would test it. The regeneration
calculus removes the objection's allelic-supply half outright
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:hlares}); its deep-root half stands or falls with a
corrected count that neither side has produced, and until one exists the
genetic data contain no demonstration that any balanced locus requires
more pre-split roots than the four copies the couple carries.

\subsection{The endowment budget for deep alleles}
One component of the HLA argument requires the endowment explicitly: the
\emph{depth} of the retained lineages. Human and chimpanzee HLA alleles
share lineages whose sequence divergence corresponds to many millions of
years; under sole progenitorship such divergence, if real rather than
conversion-generated, must reside in the founders' four copies. The
information budget is trivial: the HLA-class coding targets total roughly
$10^4$--$10^5$ bp across their loci, so endowing the couple with
chimp-depth divergence at every balanced locus costs less than
$10^{-4}$ of the genome-wide endowment budget already spent on
Eq.~\eqref{eq:depth}. The deep-allele component of the objection is
therefore not resource-limited; its evidential weight rests on the two
countable questions --- how many independent pre-split roots the allelic
genealogies require (the lineage-count test of Section~\ref{sec:roots})
and how much of the apparent depth is conversion-generated --- and both
are settled by sequence evidence, not by rhetoric.

\section{Archaic admixture and the descendant-clade extension}\label{sec:scope}

Sole progenitorship is a claim about \emph{all} modern DNA, and modern
genomes contain one component whose provenance must be treated separately:
the introgressed archaic fraction --- some $2$--$6\%$ of non-African nuclear
DNA derives from Neanderthal and Denisovan sources
\citep{Sankararaman2012,Sankararaman2014,Pruefer2014}. This section extends
the EP model to that component. The extension is affirmative: under it, the
introgressed tracts are not external DNA but couple-descended DNA returning
to the modern branch through the archaic relatives, and the admixture event
ceases to bound the claim.

\subsection{The extension}
The \emph{descendant-clade extension (DCE)} models the Neanderthal and
Denisovan lineages as descendant branches of the founding couple's
descendant population. The couple founds the metapopulation at $T_0$; its
descendant branches separate into the modern-human stem and the archaic
clades at a branching time coincident with (or immediately after) the
founding; and the later admixture between the branches --- dated to
$47$--$65$ kya on the direct clock \citep{Sankararaman2012} --- is gene flow
\emph{within} the couple's pedigree. Under the DCE every nucleotide of every
modern human genome, non-introgressed and introgressed alike, descends from
the couple.

\begin{theorem}[Descendant-clade coverage]\label{thm:scope}
Let ``full coverage'' mean that every nucleotide of every modern human
genome descends from the founding couple. Then:
(i) full coverage holds if the archaic clades branch from the couple's
descendant population; the branching condition requires $T_0$ to be at
least as old as the youngest published direct-clock estimates of the
archaic--modern population split ($550$--$765$ kya \citep{Pruefer2014};
$737$ ka \citealp{Rogers2020}), giving the descendant-clade window
$T_0 \in [550, 595]$ kya --- available at the canonical point
\eqref{eq:canonical};
(ii) full coverage \emph{including the archaic clades' uniparental
compartments} is falsified by two clock-free tests --- the
modern-to-archaic mtDNA divergence exceeds the modern--modern depth by
roughly $2$--$3\times$ \citep{Green2008,Posth2017}, and the Neanderthal Y
diverges from the modern Y by a factor $2.1$ (CI $1.7$--$2.9$; $588$ vs
$275$ kya; \citealp{Mendez2016}) --- whereas single-couple descent predicts
ratio $1$ for both;
(iii) the claim scoped to the non-introgressed genome --- sole genetic
progenitorship of the $\ge 94$--$98\%$ of modern nuclear DNA not traced to
archaic introgression, plus the entirety of the maternally and paternally
inherited compartments --- is consistent with every test above at any $T_0$
in the joint window \eqref{eq:window}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Under branch descent, a modern allele and an archaic allele coalesce
either within the post-founding history of their branches or at the couple's
four founder copies; the cross-branch pairs that land on the same founder
copy coalesce exactly at $T_0$, and split estimators accordingly localize
the clade divergence at $T_0$ --- a value the published direct-clock ranges
contain at their youngest edge. The introgressed tracts, drawn from the
archaic branch, therefore descend from the couple through the branch.
(ii) Under descent of the archaic uniparentals, every pairwise mtDNA
divergence (modern--modern and modern--archaic) equals $2\mu_{mt} T_0$
exactly, and likewise for the Y; the observed ratios of roughly $2$--$3$
contradict this at any clock scaling, because the ratios are
divergence-level facts independent of rate calibration
\citep{Green2008,Mendez2016}. (iii) At any window $T_0$ the introgression
tracts postdate the couple; under scope (iii) they are treated as external,
and all diversity constraints of Sections~\ref{sec:endo}--\ref{sec:poly}
hold for the non-introgressed genome.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The archaic-branch prediction}
The DCE makes a quantitative prediction that no external-admixture reading
of the introgressed fraction makes: the archaic branches, having passed
through the founding bottleneck and remained small, carry de-novo diversity
only. A descendant branch of effective size $N_b$ loses its endowment at
retention $e^{-\Lambda_b}$ with $\Lambda_b = T_b/(2N_b)$; at $N_b = 500$
the retention over a $450$-kyr branch is $1.5\times10^{-8}$ ---
effectively zero --- and the branch heterozygosity is de novo,
$2\mu E[T_2] \approx 4\mu N_b$. Figure~\ref{fig:branch} sweeps the branch
sizes: the modern branch (plateau $3\times10^4$) retains $47\%$ of the
endowment and reaches $\pi \approx 10^{-3}$, while branches of
$N_b \approx 10^3$--$10^4$ sit an order of magnitude lower ---
bracketing the archaic genomes' observed heterozygosities, several-fold
below present-day human levels \citep{Meyer2012,Pruefer2014}. The same
picture is independently supported from two directions: the
Neanderthal--Denisovan ancestors themselves passed through an extreme
bottleneck of effective size near $500$ \citep{Rogers2020}, and SFS-based
inference recognizes a severe reduction to $\sim$1,280 breeding
individuals near $930$--$813$ kya in the ancestry of modern humans
\citep{Hu2023}. The contrast between the branches is the prediction: one
large branch keeps the endowment, the small branches lose it --- and the
observed diversity pattern of modern versus archaic genomes follows.

\subsection{The uniparental boundary}
The clock-free tests of Theorem~\ref{thm:scope}(ii) are the DCE's stated
boundary, and the literature has already proposed the mechanism that
defines it. The Neanderthal Y lineage is absent from modern humans, and its
deep divergence is consistent with replacement in the archaic branch after
the split \citep{Mendez2016}; the deeply divergent mitochondrial genome of
the Hohlenstein--Stadel femur indicates gene flow \emph{into} the
Neanderthal branch after its separation from the modern stem
\citep{Posth2017}. Under the DCE these published proposals describe the
model's one external compartment: the archaic branches' uniparental
lineages were replaced after branching and leave no trace in modern genomes
--- no archaic mitochondrial or Y-chromosomal lineage persists in living
humans \citep{Mendez2016,Posth2017} --- so the boundary touches no modern
nucleotide. The scoping is not cosmetic, and its logic deserves statement.
Under the EP class every couple-descended uniparental lineage passes
through the couple's two maternal (or two paternal) copies, so a
modern--archaic uniparental pair under couple descent coalesces at or
below $T_0$ and diverges by at most $2\mu T_0$ --- yet the clock-free
tests of Theorem~\ref{thm:scope}(ii) observe modern--archaic depths of
$2$--$3\times$ the modern--modern value. No replacement \emph{within} the
couple's pedigree can produce this, because every couple-descended
replacing lineage coalesces with modern mtDNA no deeper than $T_0$; the
replacement the tests demand is replacement \emph{from outside the
couple's descent} --- precisely the boundary the published mechanisms
mark. The consequence for the claim's scope is stated plainly: under the
EP class, sole progenitorship is a claim about \emph{modern} genomes ---
all of every living human's DNA, nuclear and uniparental --- and cannot
be extended to the archaic clades' uniparental compartments, which are
the model's one genuinely external component. What the boundary does
establish, clock-free, is that the archaic clades' uniparental
compartments were not the founding couple's: the descendant-clade reading
is a nuclear-genome reading, and its price is stated here rather than
hidden.

Table~\ref{tab:scoping} states the scopes and their prices. The theorem is
deliberately symmetrical in its treatment: the same tests that delimit the
sole-progenitor claim also delimit what any origin model must explain about
the archaic compartments. It is worth noting the symmetry's consequence for
the debate's rhetoric: a couple model that incorporates descendant-clade
admixture --- precisely scope S2 --- treats the deep-archaic-uniparental
signals that scope S3 excludes as the same replacement phenomenon the
standard model invokes for them. The $2$--$6\%$ is a shared boundary of
both model classes, not a peculiarity of either; under the DCE, however,
the boundary no longer removes the introgressed fraction from the claim.

\section{Theoretical and empirical existence proofs}\label{sec:emp}
The feasibility theorem asserts compatibility, not mechanism; its
plausibility rests on the existence proofs --- theoretical results and
observed populations showing that single-pair origins carry and generate
large diversity. Table~\ref{tab:anchors} collects them. The theoretical
anchors are classical: \citet{Nei1975} showed that a bottleneck of a
single pair followed by rapid growth loses little diversity;
\citet{BartonCharlesworth1984} that a population founded by a single
fertilized female and doubling each generation retains over half its
heterozygosity; \citet{SlatkinExcoffier2012} that two randomly chosen
individuals carry on average three quarters of their population's
heterozygosity --- the sampled-couple coefficient that the EP model
replaces with the endowment. The empirical anchors are severe
bottlenecks that retained diversity and adaptive capacity: the European
ash-dieback invasion founded by two highly divergent individuals
\citep{McMullan2018}; an island mouflon population founded by a single
pair that retains unexpected heterozygosity \citep{Kaeuffer2007}; the
worldwide invasion of the veined rapa whelk through a dramatic bottleneck
\citep{Xue2018}; experimental populations passing through severe
founder-size bottlenecks with long-term fitness consequences that do not
prevent persistence \citep{Szucs2017}; and the vertebrate cases --- the
Mauritius kestrel restored from a single wild pair
\citep{Ewing2008}, the pink pigeon bottlenecked to roughly ten individuals
and demographically recovered with documented genomic erosion
\citep{Jackson2022}, the Chatham black robin from five
founders \citep{vonSeth2022}, the northern elephant seal from a
twenty-to-thirty count to hundreds of thousands
\citep{Hoelzel2024}. In the human lineage itself, severe archaic-era
bottlenecks have been independently inferred: an effective size near $500$
for the Neanderthal--Denisovan ancestors \citep{Rogers2020} and a
reduction to $\sim$1,280 breeding individuals near $930$--$813$ kya in the
ancestry of modern humans \citep{Hu2023} --- the recognition that the
human pedigree passes through extreme constriction is no longer exotic.
None of the non-human cases is a human case, and none purports to
be; their role is to certify that each quantitative step of the EP model
--- carrying, transmission, retention, expansion, regeneration --- has
been observed to operate at full strength in natural populations.

\section{The simulation program}\label{sec:sims}
Every number in the paper is emitted by a single fixed-seed script,
\texttt{sole\_progenitor\_model.py}, whose fourteen sections correspond to
the paper's quantitative claims: endowment capacity bounds (A);
four-allele transmission (B); the master equation engine --- exact
hazard-weighted epoch integration for $D_{<c}$ and $\Lambda_c$ (C); the
$540$-point feasibility grid (D); the compression identity and joint
window (E); the uniparental clock scan (F); the site-frequency spectrum
and Tajima's $D$ by coalescent simulation (msprime) of the de-novo
component plus the analytic backbone, with the backbone's segregating
fraction from forward Wright--Fisher simulation (G); the
linkage-disequilibrium profile by haplotype recursion with
hazard-equivalent time dilation (H); the pairwise coalescence-rate profile
and its PSMC-style inversion (I); the balanced-locus retention and
regeneration calculus (J); the descendant-clade scoping tests (K); the
empirical anchors (L); the descendant-clade archaic-branch sweep (N); and
the figures and summary outputs (M). Fixed seeds cover every
stochastic component; the complete run executes in under three minutes on
one core. \begin{sloppypar}Outputs: \texttt{feasibility\_grid.csv},
