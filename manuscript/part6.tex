\section{Derivations}\label{app:derivations}

\subsection{Endowment capacity by site configuration}\label{app:capacity}
For a site polymorphic in the couple, the four gene copies realize one of
four configurations (founders disjoint, $h_A = h_E = h$). Writing the
copy frequencies and the pool heterozygosity
$H = 1 - \sum_i p_i^2$:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
\toprule
Configuration & Copy frequencies & Per-site $H$ & Density \\
\midrule
heterozygous in one founder only & $(1/4,\,1/4,\,1/4,\,1/4)^{c}$: two distinct & $0.375$ & $2h$ total \\
same two alleles in both founders & $(1/2,\,1/2)$ & $0.500$ & overlap \\
three alleles (one shared) & $(1/2,\,1/4,\,1/4)$ & $0.625$ & overlap \\
four distinct alleles & $(1/4,\,1/4,\,1/4,\,1/4)$ & $0.750$ & overlap \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For largely disjoint founders the dominant contribution is the
single-founder-heterozygous class, giving
$H_{\mathrm{pool}} \approx 2h \times 0.375 = 0.75h$: the Definition
\ref{def:endowment} coefficient. The bound
$H_{\mathrm{pool}} \le 0.75\,h_{\mathrm{chimp}} = 1.43\times10^{-3}$
follows from the chimpanzee value
\citep{PradoMartinez2013}; the canonical $h = 1.576\times10^{-3}$ sits at
$83\%$ of that bound.

\subsection{Master-equation epoch integrals}\label{app:integrals}
With the backward size function of Section~\ref{sec:sims}, the cumulative
hazard to the couple generation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appLam}
\Lambda_c \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_{g=0}^{\gamma\text{-phase}}\frac{1}{2\cdot 2\gamma^{g}}}_{\text{expansion}}
\;+\; \frac{T_0 - G - T_{\mathrm{rec}}}{2\Ne^{\max}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{t<T_{\mathrm{rec}}}\frac{1}{2N(t)}}_{\text{recent growth}} ,
\end{equation}
with $G$ the expansion length and $T_{\mathrm{rec}}$ the recent-growth
epoch. At the canonical point the three terms are $0.417$, $0.333$, and
$0.007$ respectively, totaling $\Lambda_c = 0.759$. The depth integral
\eqref{eq:Dbelow} evaluates per epoch; pairs coalescing during the
expansion contribute depths clustered a few generations below $T_0$
(where the hazard concentrates), pairs in the plateau contribute the
truncated-exponential mean $\approx \Ne^{\max}\,(1-(1+\lambda
T)e^{-\lambda T})/(1-e^{-\lambda T})$ at $\lambda T \approx 0.33$,
and their weighted sum gives $D_{<c} = 8{,}311$ generations. The
three-way consistency of Section~\ref{sec:canonical} (master equation
$= $ msprime de-novo $+$ analytic backbone, all at $1.00\times10^{-3}$)
is the numerical verification of these integrals.

\subsection{The clock-free depth ratios}\label{app:ratios}
The scoping theorem's test (i) uses divergence \emph{levels}, which no
clock scaling touches. The first complete Neanderthal mitochondrial genome
carries $206$ fixed differences from modern human mtDNAs
\citep{Green2008}, and the Hohlenstein--Stadel lineage is deeper still
\citep{Posth2017}, while the deepest modern--modern mtDNA divergence is
$\sim$$60$--$100$ substitutions --- a ratio of $\sim$$2$--$3$. Under
archaic-descent-from-the-couple, every extant mtDNA lineage would
coalesce at the founding mother, making the two divergence classes
\emph{equal}; the observed gap is possible only if the
Neanderthal mitochondrial lineage is external to the couple (or was
replaced in the archaic branch after split --- the replacement proposed
for the Y chromosome by \citealp{Mendez2016} and for the mitochondrial
lineage by \citealp{Posth2017}). The same arithmetic holds
for the Y chromosome, where the Neanderthal lineage diverges from the
modern Y by a factor $2.1$ (CI $1.7$--$2.9$; TMRCA $588$ vs.\ $275$ kya)
\citep{Mendez2016}. Because
both ratios compare divergences \emph{within the same molecule class},
they are invariant under any rescaling $s$ of the molecular clock ---
hence ``clock-free.''

\subsection{The ancestral-rate correction factor}\label{app:sfactor}
The correction factor of Section~\ref{sec:uni} is the published
mutation-rate discrepancy, made explicit. The human--chimpanzee nuclear
divergence is $d \approx 1.2$--$1.3\%$. At the paleontological split
time $T_{\mathrm{split}} \approx 6.5$ Myr and 25-year generations, the
implied per-generation rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:muphy}
\mu_{\mathrm{phy}} \;=\; \frac{d/2}{T_{\mathrm{split}}/25\,\mathrm{yr}}
\;\approx\; \frac{0.0062}{2.6\times10^{5}}
\;\approx\; 2.4\times10^{-8},
\end{equation}
whereas the direct trio-measured rate is
$\mu_{\mathrm{trio}} = 1.0$--$1.3\times10^{-8}$
\citep{Scally2012,Jonsson2017} --- a ratio
$s = \mu_{\mathrm{phy}}/\mu_{\mathrm{trio}} \approx 2$. Clocks anchored
on the split inherit the faster ancestral rate and therefore
\emph{underestimate} recent coalescence dates by $\approx s$; applying
the correction to the published mtDNA and Y estimates is the scan of
Table~\ref{tab:clockscan}. The direct-clock quantities of this paper
($\pi$, $E[T_2]$, TMR4A, LD) are measured on pedigrees and are not
rescaled.

\section{Reproduction}\label{app:repro}
The complete suite is the single script
\texttt{sole\_progenitor\_model.py} (Python 3.12; numpy, scipy,
matplotlib, msprime). It executes end to end in $\approx 3$ minutes on
one core (\texttt{python3 sole\_progenitor\_model.py}); the
\texttt{--quick} flag reduces replicate counts for a $\approx$20-second
check. All stochastic components use fixed seeds (coalescent:
$20260101+$; backbone: $99001$; linkage disequilibrium: $88001$;
balanced-locus: $66001$; coalescence-rate profile: $770001+$), so every
number in this paper is bit-reproducible. The
outputs directory contains
\texttt{feasibility\_grid.csv} (540 rows), \texttt{clock\_scan.csv},
\texttt{sfs\_endowed\_pair.csv}, \texttt{ld\_decay\_endowed\_pair.csv},
\texttt{ne\_profile\_endowed\_pair.csv},
\texttt{hla\_regeneration.csv}, \texttt{scoping\_tests.csv},
\texttt{empirical\_anchors.csv}, \texttt{archaic\_branch.csv},
\texttt{feasibility\_summary.json},
\texttt{feasibility\_report.txt}, and the eight figures.
